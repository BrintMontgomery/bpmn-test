# OFC-002 — Nursing Intakes Consumer

## Actors

* Admissions Coordinator  
* Medical Records Staff  
* Security Officer  
* Unit Nurse  
* Charge Aide  
* Mental Health Technician (MHT)  
* Consumer  
* Social Worker  
* Physician  
* Health Information Coordinator  
* Pharmacy Staff  
* Dietary Manager  
* Originating Jail Staff  
* Legal Counsel  

## Context

The Oklahoma Forensic Center requires a standardized nursing admission process to receive a consumer after security intake, complete initial physical and behavioral assessments, coordinate the physician admission, obtain and distribute required forms, enter nursing information into Avatar, and establish the consumer’s nursing record for ongoing treatment.

## Preconditions

* Scheduled admission email from the Admissions Coordinator, normally sent approximately one day before the planned admission.  
* Admission email identifies the consumer’s name, originating county, expected arrival time, assigned unit, assigned Physician, and order-to-treat status and includes an attached consumer report or information sheet.  
* Medical Records Staff provide available information concerning an existing Avatar number or prior OFC or facility admission.  
* Confirmed destination unit.  
* Unit Nurse assigned to the admission; the charge nurse of the day normally serves as the default admitting nurse \[a\].  
* Mental Health Technician available for the admission and escort process.  
* Active Avatar access for the Unit Nurse.  
* Nursing Admit Pack is printed on the day of the confirmed admission.\[p\]  
* Additional admission documents are accessible as applicable.\[q\]  
* Consumer consent documents are made available.\[r\]  
* Consumer informational materials are made available.\[s\]

## Outcome

The nursing admission is complete when required nursing and physician paperwork has been collated and reviewed, required paper records have been filed in the consumer chart, required clinical information has been entered into Avatar, and necessary pharmacy, dietary, physician, and social-work handoffs have occurred. The Health Information Coordinator performs quality-control review of nursing documentation and Avatar entries, while the paper chart remains available for ongoing treatment and later scanning according to health-information procedures.

## Main Path

1. **Admissions Coordinator** sends the scheduled admission email (approximately one day before arrival).\[t\]  
2. **Medical Records Staff** send Nursing available information concerning an existing Avatar number, previous OFC admission, or admission at another facility.  
3. **Unit Nurse** or **Charge Aide** prints the Nursing Admit Pack (on the day of the confirmed admission) \[b\].  
4. **Unit Nurse** verbally requests an available **Mental Health Technician (MHT)** to assist with the admission (earlier in the admission day).  
5. **Security Officer** calls the destination and reports that the consumer is ready for the nursing admission process.  
6. **Unit Nurse** reports to the admissions area and begins the nursing admission.  
7. **Unit Nurse** performs the required lice check and treatment.  
8. **Unit Nurse** interviews the **Consumer** and begins the Nursing Unit Biophysical Assessment, including the scarring and tattoo sheet.  
9. **Mental Health Technician (MHT)** assists with the shower process according to staffing and supervision requirements.  
10. **Consumer** completes the shower.  
11. **Security Officer** and **Mental Health Technician (MHT)** escort the **Consumer** from the shower area to the assigned unit.  
12. **Unit Nurse** receives the **Consumer** on the unit and obtains a complete set of vital signs.  
13. **Unit Nurse** updates the Nursing Unit Biophysical Assessment and records required vital-sign information.  
14. **Unit Nurse** completes the nursing trauma assessment questions and the initial suicide-risk assessment.  
15. **Unit Nurse** completes Steps 1 and 2 of the SAFE-T Protocol Signature Page.  
16. **Unit Nurse** or **Social Worker** contacts the **Physician** when the Consumer is ready for the physician admission encounter.\[u\]  
17. **Physician** interviews the **Consumer** concerning medical history, mental-health history, trauma, suicide risk, substance use, current condition, and other admission issues.  
18. **Physician** completes the physician admission paperwork and completes Steps 3 through 5 of the SAFE-T Protocol Signature Page.  
19. **Unit Nurse** completes the Medication Reconciliation Form with the **Physician**. \[c\]  
20. **Unit Nurse** offers the **Consumer** the required consent documents after the physician encounter and records signatures.  
21. **Consumer** signs consent documents.\[d\]  
22. **Physician** reviews the Consent for Treatment with Psychoactive Medication document with the **Consumer** when psychoactive medication is prescribed or proposed.  
23. **Mental Health Technician (MHT)** orients the **Consumer** to the assigned unit (by showing the Consumer the room, showers, restrooms, desk area, and other relevant unit locations.)  
24. **Mental Health Technician (MHT)** gives the **Consumer** the informational materials (and explains that they are informational rather than mandatory consent documents.)  
25. **Unit Nurse** collates the completed nursing, physician, and applicable security paperwork into the admission record.  
26. **Unit Nurse** reviews each required document for completeness, identifies missing information or blank required fields, and obtains corrections when available.  
27. **Unit Nurse** routes required medication-related documentation or information to Pharmacy Staff.  
28. **Pharmacy Staff** receives the medication information required for the consumer’s medication-management process.  
29. **Unit Nurse** routes the Nutritional Screening to the Dietary Manager through the established unit process.  
30. **Dietary Manager** receives the Nutritional Screening for dietary tracking and follow-up.  
31. **Unit Nurse** or **Charge Aide** files the completed paper admission documents in the Consumer’s chart.  
32. **Unit Nurse** enters the nursing admission information into Avatar (this includes the majority of applicable information collected on paper.)  
33. **Unit Nurse** completes the Avatar IP General Note. (This includes the basic admission narrative, observed condition, cooperation with the admission process, relevant medical observations, and legal status.)  
34. **Unit Nurse** completes the Avatar Nursing Assessment (This includes available medical history, current medications, drug and alcohol history, and other required nursing information.)  
35. **Unit Nurse** completes the Avatar OK Trauma Assessment using information obtained during the admission interview and available supporting documentation.  
36. **Unit Nurse** completes the Avatar OK Fall Assessment (This includes fall history, ambulatory aids, gait or mobility concerns, mental status, and other applicable fall-risk information.)  
37. **Unit Nurse** completes the Avatar OK Daily Functioning Assessment. (This includes information available at admission concerning hygiene, bathing, dressing, mobility, food intake and preparation, use of utensils or appliances, orientation, money use, basic cognitive functioning, and related activities of daily living.)  
38. **Unit Nurse** verifies completion of the nursing admission during the admitting shift whenever practicable.\[e\]  
39. **Health Information Coordinator** audits the nursing paperwork, Avatar documentation, and applicable progress notes for completeness and quality-control purposes.  
40. **Unit Nurse** completes the nursing admission and communicates unresolved issues.\[v\]  

## Options from main path:

### Option A — Female Consumer and Same-Sex Staffing

Trigger: The Consumer is female and same-sex staffing is required for the search, shower, or related personal-care activities.

1. Unit Nurse coordinates with Security Officer to confirm available female staff.  
2. A female Mental Health Technician or other authorized female staff member assists when a female Security Officer is not available.  
3. Staff maintain the same-sex supervision requirements during the applicable search, shower, or personal-care activity.  
4. Resume the Main Path at the appropriate assessment or escort step.  

### Option B — Aggressive or Unsafe Consumer

Trigger: The Consumer is violent, aggressive, highly agitated, or presents an immediate behavioral safety concern during nursing admission.

1. Unit Nurse assesses the immediate clinical and safety concern.  
2. Unit Nurse contacts the Physician and informs the Security Officer when additional security precautions are required.  
3. Physician determines whether immediate medication or another clinical intervention is required.  
4. Security Officer adjusts movement or staffing precautions according to the clinical direction.  
5. Resume the Main Path when the Unit Nurse and Physician determine that routine admission activities can continue.  

### Option C — Significant Medical Exception

Trigger: Known or observed medical needs may exceed OFC’s available level of care or make routine admission medically unsafe.

1. Unit Nurse assesses the Consumer’s current medical condition and available medical records.  
2. Unit Nurse immediately informs the Physician of the medical concern.  
3. Admissions Coordinator coordinates any required admission-status decision with clinical leadership.  
4. Legal Counsel participates when acceptance, diversion, court-order, or legal-custody questions require legal review.  
5. Physician directs emergency evaluation or hospital diversion when the Consumer cannot safely be managed at OFC.  
6. Do not continue the routine Main Path until the medical disposition is resolved.  

### Option D — Medication History Is Unavailable

Trigger: The Physician, Unit Nurse, and Consumer cannot establish the Consumer’s current or recent medication regimen.

1. Unit Nurse informs the Physician that the medication history is incomplete.  
2. Social Worker contacts Originating Jail Staff when the Physician requests additional medication information.  
3. Originating Jail Staff provide available medication information.  
4. Unit Nurse updates the Medication Reconciliation Form with verified information.  
5. Resume the Main Path at the medication-reconciliation step.  

### Option E — Consumer Refuses Consent Documents

Trigger: The Consumer declines to sign one or more consent documents.

1. Unit Nurse explains the applicable document and offers the Consumer the opportunity to sign.  
2. Consumer may refuse any of the voluntary consent documents.  
3. Unit Nurse records the refusal according to the applicable form or documentation process. (Note: refusal of a voluntary consent not treated as completion of a signed authorization.)  
4. Unit Nurse continues the remainder of the admission when the refusal does not create a separate legal or clinical barrier.  

### Option F — Consumer Cannot Meaningfully Participate

Trigger: The Consumer is unable or unwilling to participate effectively in questioning, consent review, or informational activities because of current behavior or mental status.

1. Unit Nurse completes the clinical activities that can safely be performed.  
2. Unit Nurse documents the Consumer’s level of cooperation and observed condition.  
3. Unit Nurse leaves unavailable historical information unresolved rather than inventing responses.  
4. Unit Nurse or Social Worker returns to unresolved questions or documents when clinically appropriate.  
5. Unit Nurse continues required custody, safety, and treatment activities that do not depend on voluntary participation.  

### Option G — Rush Admission

Trigger: OFC knows that the Consumer is expected but receives substantially less advance notice than usual.

1. Unit Nurse uses an available preprinted admission packet when necessary.  
2. Unit Nurse confirms the current admission information before using the packet.  
3. Unit Nurse follows the standard Main Path after the Consumer arrives. (i.e., no separate abbreviated nursing process is used solely because the admission is rushed.)  

### Option H — Multiple Consumers Arrive Together

Trigger: Two or more Consumers require admission processing during the same period.

1. Security Officer maintains waiting Consumers in the available secure holding area.  
2. Unit Nurse processes each Consumer in turn as staffing and admission-area capacity permit.  
3. Mental Health Technician assignments are adjusted according to unit staffing needs.  

### Option I — Changed or Rescheduled Admission

Trigger: The planned date, arrival time, unit, Physician, or other admission information changes after the initial notification.

1. Admissions Coordinator distributes the updated admission information to the established recipients.  
2. Unit Nurse does not rely on a final Nursing Admit Pack printed for an earlier admission date.  
3. Unit Nurse or Charge Aide prints or updates the Nursing Admit Pack on the actual confirmed admission day.  
4. Unit Nurse resumes the Main Path at the preparation step.  

### Option J — After-Hours Admission

Trigger: A Consumer arrives outside the usual daytime admission period.

1. Unit Nurse follows the standard nursing admission process.  
2. Unit Nurse coordinates required Physician, Security Officer, and Mental Health Technician availability.  
3. Unit Nurse completes the admission during the admitting shift whenever practicable.  

## Notes

\[a\] The charge nurse assigned to the admission unit on the day of admission normally serves as the default admitting Unit Nurse. Charge Techs are higher-ranking Mental Health Technicians and may perform MHT functions when assigned.

\[b\] Print the Nursing Admit Pack on the confirmed admission day rather than solely in response to the advance email because admission dates and arrival times may change. The interview established no required email subject-line format or threading convention.

\[c\] Medication history may come from the Physician, Consumer, originating jail, legal documentation, legal records, or other available records. Nurses do not enter an unverified medication as established fact when the information cannot be confirmed.  

\[d\] The Consumer may refuse the consent documents. The informational materials provided by the Mental Health Technician are distinct from consent documents and are provided for consumer information. The Consent for Treatment with Psychoactive Medication requires coordination with the Physician.

\[e\] The interview described completion before the 7:00 p.m. shift change as normal operational practice, not as a formally cited regulatory SLA. Routine admissions are expected to be completed before the 7:00 p.m. shift change 

\[f\] OFC does not normally schedule admissions on weekends or holidays. After-hours admissions were described as rare and as following the same substantive process.

\[g\] The Unit Nurse uses paper documentation during portions of the consumer-facing assessment because Avatar is not normally available at the point of interview. Nurses may use a locally developed trauma-question reference sheet to avoid missing Avatar-only questions. The interview did not establish that this reference sheet is an official controlled OFC form.

\[h\] Avatar and the paper packet do not contain identical fields. Some paper information is entered into Avatar, some Avatar questions have no corresponding paper form, and some paper information has no corresponding Avatar field. Do not create or infer information merely to populate an electronic field.

\[i\] Nursing paperwork is not normally scanned into Avatar immediately at admission. Health Information personnel may scan portions of thick charts during the stay, and remaining paper records are scanned as part of the discharge record process. Paper records may be destroyed after authorized scanning according to health-information procedures.

\[j\] The Health Information Coordinator performs quality-control review by auditing nursing documentation, Avatar entries, progress notes, and supporting paperwork. This review is a documentation-control activity and does not replace the Unit Nurse’s responsibility for completing the admission record.

\[k\] Interview participants indicated that many admission documents may relate to Joint Commission requirements, but no specific Joint Commission standard, OFC policy number, statute, or other controlling authority was identified during the interview. Do not cite a specific compliance authority for an individual form until the governing requirement is verified.

\[l\] The Nursing Unit Biophysical Assessment guides the admission assessment, there is no separate nursing checklist or policy that specifies every examination action. Nurses also apply professional nursing assessment standards, including assessment of relevant body systems.

\[m\] The Vendor Payee Form originates in the security process. Nursing may receive security paperwork when collating the complete admission record, but responsibility for completing the Vendor Payee Form belongs to the security workflow rather than the nursing workflow.

\[n\] The Nutritional Screening is physically associated with the security-originated packet in the process described during the interview, but Security does not complete it. The form proceeds to the unit and Dietary Manager for dietary tracking.

\[o\] The nursing admission overlaps with physician and social-work admission activities. Completion of this nursing intake SOP does not mean that social-work intake, case-management intake, competency-restoration services, treatment, or discharge processes are complete.

\[p\]  Nursing packet contains:

* Routine Urinalysis.  
* Drug and Nicotine Screening.  
* Miscellaneous Request — next-day laboratory work.  
* Miscellaneous Request — 12-week laboratory work.  
* Medication Administration Record.  
* Medication Administration STAT and PRN.  
* Nursing Unit Biophysical Assessment, including the scarring and tattoo sheet.  
* Consumer Medication Education.

\[q\] Documentation should be available as needed:

* Vendor Payee Form received through the security process.  
* Admission Property Inventory.  
* Admission Physician Order Sheet.  
* Physician Orders Form.  
* Initial Treatment Plan on Admission.  
* Nutritional Screening.  
* SAFE-T Protocol Signature Page.  
* Medication Reconciliation Form.

\[r\] Consumer documents are as follows:

* Consent for Release of Confidential or Protected Information.  
* Consent for Treatment with Psychoactive Medication.  
* Telemedicine Consent/Refusal.  
* Designation of Treatment Advocate.  
* Special Behavioral Procedures.  
* Advance Directive for Health Care.  
* Acknowledgement of Receipt of Notice of Privacy Practices.

\[s\] Consumer information documents are as follows:

* Consumer Rights and Responsibilities.  
* Advance Directive Questions and Answers.  
* Consumer Responsibilities.  
* Consumer Grievance Procedure.  
* General Information — Infection Control.  
* Information on Seclusion and Restraint.  
* Medical Emergencies.  
* Notice of Privacy Practices.

\[t\] This email is sent to security, nurse managers, nurse coordinators, Pharmacy, Psychology, Social Work, Supply, Medical Records, the admitting Physician, and other established operational recipients. The email identifies the consumer, county, expected arrival time, assigned unit, assigned Physician, and order-to-treat status and includes the available consumer information attachment.

\[u\] The Social Worker may arrange the telemedicine session when the Physician is remote.

\[v\] This may include clinical, medication, behavioral, or support issues to the Physician, Social Worker, or other responsible treatment staff for continued action.
